**An open series of books for engineers who have worked in the field and are now moving into modern geospatial engineering.**

## Why this series

The world of geodata has changed enormously in recent years: point clouds, satellite time series, cloud data, Python, machine learning. Education hasn't kept up. There's plenty of learning material out there, but nearly all of it is about tools, not about the work an engineer actually does.

An engineer has to be able to solve a real problem. That means learning to ask the right questions, so we can break the problem down into concrete tasks and find the solutions, methods and tools for each one. It means finding data and checking it, knowing whether we can trust a result, and knowing what to do when something doesn't work.

Each book in the series is a real project, from the first question to a checked result. We learn the tools from scratch, for the specific tasks the project needs. It's simply a more effective way to learn.

## How the books work

- **One book, one real project.** Open data, northern Spain.
- **No repetition.** Whatever we've learnt in an earlier book, we just apply from then on.
- **Written by someone who is learning too.** A chapter appears once the author has worked through the topic and understood it. That's why the books include real difficulties, real mistakes and how they were solved.
- **Honest checks.** If a result doesn't hold up, that gets published too.
- **Spanish and English.** Text under CC BY 4.0, code under Apache-2.0, all of it free.

## The path: seven books

| No. | Book | What it teaches | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [PyQGIS from Scratch](https://elviragd-lab.github.io/field-to-code-pyqgis/) | Python from scratch, automating QGIS, building your own plugin | in progress |
| 2 | [Remote Sensing from Scratch](https://elviragd-lab.github.io/field-to-code-remote-sensing/) | how a satellite sees the Earth: Sentinel-2, indices, terrain, accuracy | in progress |
| 3 | Spatial Statistics from Scratch | statistics for spatial data in Python | coming soon |
| 4 | [LiDAR from Scratch](https://elviragd-lab.github.io/field-to-code-lidar/) | point clouds and forest growth from Spain's national LiDAR | in progress |
| 5 | Cloud Native | cloud data and satellite image time series | planned |
| 6 | Storms | storms, forest damage and power outages | planned |
| 7 | Risk | where trees will fall on power lines: the model and how we test it | planned |

Books 4–7 are the learning side of **Pajares**, an open research project on forests, storms and power lines in Spain. The project's first tool is [`canopy-growth`](https://github.com/ElviraGD-lab/canopy-growth) ([DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23856842](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23856842)).

## About the author

Elvira Gayanova, surveying engineer. She spent five years with a forest inventory expedition in the mountains of the Southern Urals, then ran her own surveying practice in the Netherlands and Spain for fifteen years. After a break to raise three children, she is coming back to the profession and learning it afresh: the Master's in Geotechnology and GIS Project Development at the University of Oviedo (2026–2027), and this series, an open account of the journey.
